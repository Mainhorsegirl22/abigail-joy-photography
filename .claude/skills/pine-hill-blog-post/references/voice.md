# How the owner writes, and how to sound like her

She is the breeder. She raises the puppies in her kitchen, trains Freda in scent work and SAR, answers every email herself. The posts are the phone call written down.

## Openings she would write

- "Most of the phone calls we get start the same way."
- "This checklist exists because of a call we get every litter, usually the evening of go home day, usually about a crate that turned out to be the wrong size."
- "Working line German Shepherd puppies are not the same animal as the shepherd most people picture from a movie."

Not: "German Shepherds are one of the most popular and versatile breeds in the world."

## Her own edit of a paragraph I wrote

Mine: "At seven weeks we temperament test every puppy and match on that, not on colour or sex. The puppy with the most drive in the litter goes to the sport or search and rescue home. The steadiest, softest one goes to the family with small kids."

Hers: "At seven weeks we temperament test every puppy. The puppy with the most drive in the litter goes to the home best suited for them each one to their own families based on personality! Why not let families choose their own? As the breeder I have spent every single moment of the 8 weeks old with our puppies, watching working and observing them. Most unexperienced dog owners won't be able to determine a puppies temperament in just a 30 minute window of time."

What that tells you: she switches to "I" when it is her call. She asks the reader a question and answers it. She uses an exclamation point when she means it. She writes numerals ("8 weeks", "30 minute"). She does not polish every sentence, and the post is better for it. Match that. Do not clean her up when editing.

## Sentence habits

- Short declaratives next to one long one. "Ours sleep on the kitchen floor most of the day. Freda will swim, hike, do a bite session and an hour of obedience in the morning, then come in and lie down while the kids step over her."
- A verdict at the end of a paragraph: "Meet the parents." "That is your answer." "Every day."
- Second person is fine: "you will not need us to point it out."
- Specific numbers and places: seven weeks, day three to day sixteen, an hour north of Bangor, twenty minutes on the phone.
- She hedges honestly: "Often, not always."

## Things that read as AI, avoid

- Matched triplets ("intelligence, loyalty, and drive") more than once.
- "Not X, but Y" more than once.
- "It is important to note", "in today's world", "when it comes to", "a testament to", "unparalleled", "delve".
- A closing paragraph that summarises the post.
- Every paragraph the same length.
- Emoji, bold lead-ins on every bullet, headings that are all questions.
- Captions on every photo. She captions some and leaves others alone.

## Image and alt habits

She puts a photo of the actual dog near the top, sometimes a two-image gallery. Alt text is a short phrase, lowercase "shepherd" is fine: "working line German shepherd", "show line vrs working line". Keep the keyword in at least one alt, plainly.
